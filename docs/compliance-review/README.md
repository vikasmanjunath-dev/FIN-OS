# Compliance review sheets for Money Reels

Generated by `npm run reels:compliance` (do not edit by hand; work in a copy and send it back). Nothing here changes a reel.

**Scope:** 177 live reels and 50 draft reels waiting behind the review gate, each with its "How this is worked out" note, plus the 23 money facts shown at the end of an episode. The tool found **473 claims** in 161 of 227 reels (plus the money facts). 0 reels have both sign-offs in `lib/reels/review.ts`; the rest do not.

## How to use `claims.csv`

1. Each row is one sentence of one on-screen text that states something a reader can check: a regulator, a section, a limit, a rate, a scheme, a date or period, a helpline, or wording that could read as advice. The **Category** says why it is there; the **Figure** column shows the exact number, section or phrase found.
2. For each row, find the primary source (the Act, circular, scheme notification or official site) and write it in **Source** with the section or page and the date you checked. If the claim is wrong, out of date or too strong, put `change` or `remove` in **Verdict** and say what in **Reader note**; otherwise `OK`.
3. **Framing** matters for rates: `stated as fact` means the sentence gives a percentage for interest, inflation or returns without calling it an assumption or an example. Those are the rows most likely to need a source or a rewrite.
4. **Meant as** says whether the reel asserts the sentence (`asserted`), shows it as a wrong answer option, or shows it inside an example scam message. Only `asserted` rows are claims the reel stands behind; the others are there so you can confirm the reel labels them clearly.
5. **Auto cross-check** compares the amounts and rates with the constants the app itself uses (`lib/taxcore.ts`, `lib/taxSavings.ts`). `matches` means the sentence agrees with the app; `matches-literal` means it agrees with a number typed into the app without a shared constant; `mismatch` means it differs and is the first thing to look at; `known` is a number the tool lists as a fixed fact (the 1930 helpline); `not-checked` means the tool has nothing to compare it with. **A match with the app is not a source**: the app could be out of date too. `lib/taxcore.ts` is written for FY 2025-26.

**Known caveat** carries what the trust-note pass already found on that reel (`docs/REELS-FIGURE-FINDINGS.md`, 19 reels): `[A]` the figure is right but rests on something the reel does not say, `[B]` a rounding or consistency point, `[C]` a rule tied to a financial year that may have moved. Settle those first.

`reels-index.csv` lists every reel with its sign-off state and how many claims it has, so you can see which reels are done and which are not. `summary-by-category.csv` is the table below.

## What a sign-off means

A compliance sign-off is a line for the reel in `lib/reels/review.ts` (`REVIEWED`), added by the content owner, with the reader’s name and the date. It records that the named person:

1. read every row for that reel in `claims.csv` and watched the whole reel, so nothing was judged only from the listed sentences;
2. recorded a source for every `asserted` row, or asked for the row to be changed or removed and the change was made;
3. checked that no wording reads as a personal recommendation or a promise of return, and that figures are labelled as examples or assumptions where they are;
4. is satisfied with the year the reel names, or with its wording telling the viewer to check the current rule.

It is a record that a named person did this read, not a legal opinion and not a guarantee that the rules will not change. The tool never makes this entry, and a reel with a sign-off is only released when the Hindi sign-off is there too.

## What the tool cannot do

- It reads English text. Hindi sentences are shown beside each claim so you can see whether the figure agrees, but a Hindi-only claim is not extracted.
- It finds sentences with a number, a name or a phrase from its lists. A claim made without any of them (“the bank must refund you”) is not found. Read the reel itself for the sign-off.
- Worked tax examples (`tax-example`) are checked by the maths tests (`__tests__`, `npm run reels:check`), not here.
- Reels built from a person’s own figures (“Your Number”) fill their sentences at run time from templates in `personal*.ts`; those sentences are not in these sheets.
